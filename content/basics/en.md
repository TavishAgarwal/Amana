Being a carrier: a carrier has one changed copy of a gene and one working copy. Being a carrier usually does not mean you have symptoms yourself. A condition passed down this way appears only when a child gets a changed copy from both parents. When both parents carry a change in the same gene, there is a 1 in 4 chance for each pregnancy that a child has the condition. When only one parent carries the change, children do not have the condition, but some may be carriers. Relatives, such as cousins, are more likely to carry the same change because they share family genes. The decision is yours. A genetic counsellor can explain your options. Sources: GeneReviews, MedlinePlus (general inheritance pattern).
